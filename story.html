L = 50 mm; t = 0.5 mm; pitch = 4 mm (250 fins/m). Bare-wall h = 40 W/(m²·K); finned-wall h = 30 W/(m²·K). Compare at the same wall and air temperatures.

Take a 1 m × 1 m patch, with each fin spanning 1 m into the page. Assume pure aluminum at 300 K: k = 237 W/(m·K), from Appendix A, Table A.1. This property is not supplied in the question. Steady one-dimensional fin conduction, constant properties, perfect attachment, negligible radiation and narrow-edge losses. Corrected length accounts approximately for tip convection.

Why does the printed answer say 1640.3%?

The lecture’s finite-fin model, with k = 237 W/(m·K), gives 1317.6%. An independent convective-tip calculation agrees. The printed 1640.3% would require an efficiency of about 88.87%, versus the calculated 71.75%, with the same area accounting.

The question omits conductivity and evaluation temperature. A different property or assumption needs to be identified to justify the printed value. This walkthrough does not treat that discrepancy as a student error.
